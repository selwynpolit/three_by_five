# Cards and Stacks

Cards are the heart of 3by5. Each one is a digital 3×5 index card — compact, focused, and date-stamped. Stacks are the colored containers that hold them. Together, they give you a lightweight system for organizing projects, contexts, and the chaos of daily life without turning your task manager into a second job.


## What a card is

Every card has a **date** printed at the top in the Caveat handwriting font — the same kind of casual, analog feel you get from writing on a real index card. Below the date is an optional **project title**: a short label like "Q2 Launch" or "Kitchen reno" that reminds you what this card is about.

The body of the card is split into two columns:

- **Now** — things that need to happen today, or urgently. This is your short list.
- **Later** — things that matter but can wait. Not forgotten, just not right now.

That two-column structure is intentional. The distinction between "I need to do this today" and "I'll get to this eventually" is one of the most useful filters in task management, and having it built into every card means you're always making a small, deliberate decision about urgency.


## Creating a card

Press **⌘N** from anywhere in the app to create a new card. You can also click the **New Card** button in the top-right corner of the header. The card is stamped with today's date and added to whichever stack is currently active.

Cards are sorted by date, most recent first, so a brand-new card lands at the top of your deck. If you want a card to represent a specific date — say, you're planning for next week — you can edit the date directly on the card.


## The Stack view (flip deck)

When you open 3by5, cards are displayed in **Stack view** by default. It's designed to mimic a physical deck of index cards:

- The **draw pile** is on the left — that's the active card you're currently looking at.
- The **discard pile** is on the right — cards you've already reviewed this session.

Flip through the deck using:

- **Left/right arrow keys**
- **Trackpad swipe** left or right
- The **navigation arrow buttons** on screen

Each flip plays a smooth arc animation — the card actually flies through the air from one pile to the other. It's a small touch, but it makes the whole experience feel satisfying rather than flat.

At the bottom of the screen, a row of **dots** shows your position in the deck. Click any dot to jump directly to that card. The backs of cards show a subtle "BACK" watermark and a colored stripe at the top in your stack's color, so you always know which stack you're in.


## Other view modes

Not in the mood for a deck? The icons in the top-left header switch you between four layouts:

**Grid** tiles all your cards side by side like a corkboard. It's the right view when you want to compare cards or see everything at a glance.

**Free Canvas** gives you an open space to drag cards wherever you like. If you think spatially and like grouping related cards together, this mode is for you.

**Task List** collapses all your cards down to a flat list of tasks. No card chrome — just the work.

**Stack view** is always one click away when you want to get back to the focused, one-card-at-a-time experience.


## The card context menu

Right-click on any card header to reveal a menu of actions:

- **Hide** — removes the card from view (not deleted, just out of sight). An Undo toast appears immediately so you can change your mind.
- **Snooze until...** — pick a date and the card disappears until then.
- **Kanban view...** — opens this card's tasks in the Kanban board.
- **Move to stack...** — send the card to a different stack.
- **Carry Forward** — creates a new card dated today containing only the incomplete tasks from this card. Column assignments (Now/Later), priority, due dates, recurrence rules, and tags are all preserved. Completed tasks are not carried. The new card opens immediately so you can review it. If all tasks are already complete (or the card has none), a brief toast tells you there's nothing to carry.
- **Delete completed tasks** — a quick sweep to clear out anything you've already checked off.
- **Archive** — moves the card out of active circulation.
- **Delete** — removes the card permanently (with an Undo toast for safety).


## What is a Stack?

A Stack is a color-coded container for cards. Use stacks however they make sense to you: one per project, one per area of life, one per client, or whatever organizing principle fits your brain.

The sidebar's **STACKS** section lists all your stacks. At the very top is **All Stacks** — a master view that shows cards from every stack simultaneously. It's perfect for a morning review or whenever you want the full picture.

Clicking a stack name focuses the card view on just that stack. The active stack is highlighted with a colored left-border stripe in its stack color. Click **All Stacks** to return to the combined view.

You can also drag any card from the card view and drop it directly onto a stack name in the sidebar to move it there instantly. The target stack highlights as you drag over it.


## Creating and managing Stacks

**Adding a stack** — hover over the **STACKS** header until the **+** icon appears and click it, or right-click any stack row and choose **Add Stack**. The dialog asks for a name and lets you pick from 8 color swatches.

**Renaming a stack** — right-click its row in the sidebar and choose **Rename**, or hover over the row and click the **⋯** button that appears on the right.

**Deleting a stack** — right-click its row and choose **Delete**. A confirmation dialog gives you two options:

- **Move cards to…** — relocates all cards to another stack of your choosing (the default).
- **Delete all cards in this stack** — removes the cards along with the stack.

You can never delete the last remaining stack. If only one stack exists the Delete option is disabled.


## A note on color

The color you pick for a stack shows up in several places: the dot next to the stack name in the sidebar, the top stripe on card backs in Stack view, and the small priority/tag dots on individual tasks. A little bit of color goes a long way when you're scanning for context at a glance.
