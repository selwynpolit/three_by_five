# Tips and Workflows

3by5 is flexible enough to fit a lot of different ways of working. Here are a few patterns that work well, along with some power-user tips that are easy to miss.

---

## Morning planning routine

A five-minute morning check-in sets you up for the day. Here's a simple sequence:

1. **Open Today View (⌘4)** — see what's overdue and what's due today. If something is overdue and you're not doing it today, open the task and push the due date forward. Be honest with yourself.
2. **Open Stack View (⌘1)** — flip through your cards with the arrow keys. Get a feel for what's in play.
3. **Move tasks between Now and Later** — decide what's actually happening today and what's going to wait. Later is not a trash bin; it's a holding area for things you've captured but aren't committing to yet.
4. **Snooze anything that isn't today's problem** — if a card is irrelevant until Thursday, snooze it until Thursday morning. You'll get it back automatically, no reminders needed.

---

## GTD-style usage

3by5 maps naturally onto GTD (Getting Things Done) thinking if that's your system.

- **Cards as projects or areas** — one card per project, one card per area of life (Work, Home, Health, Side Projects, etc.)
- **Now = MIT** — your Most Important Tasks for right now, the things you've committed to doing today
- **Later = backlog** — things you've captured and want to remember, but haven't scheduled yet
- **Kanban view for multi-step projects** — if a project has distinct stages (To Do → In Progress → Pending → Done), work it on the Kanban board

A new card at the start of each planning session is a nice ritual. Date it, give it a project title, and pull your tasks onto it. Then flip through the stack when you're ready to work.

## Using the Task List for a weekly review

The Task List view (⌘1 → rightmost icon) is one of the most underused parts of 3by5 for people who haven't discovered it yet. Here's a quick weekly review using it:

1. Open the Task List
2. Enable the Show Hidden toggle in the header (the eye icon) — now you're seeing everything, including snoozed cards
3. Sort by Due Date — everything coming up lands at the top
4. Look for tasks that are overdue and decide what to do with them: reschedule, complete, or delete
5. Sort by Tag to see if any tag has a pile of tasks that needs attention
6. Sort by Priority to spot anything high-priority you might have overlooked

The three-state sort cycle (ascending → descending → unsorted) means you can always get back to the original view.

## Power user tips

**⌘K is your fastest tool.** Once you get used to global search, you'll rarely scroll looking for something. Type a word you remember and the result is usually on screen within a second.

**Right-click everything.** Cards, task rows, stack names, column headers — most of them have context menus with useful options. The right-click menu on a card gives you hide, snooze, carry forward, archive, and more. The right-click menu on a stack name lets you add, rename, or delete stacks without hunting for a button.

**The free canvas is a thinking tool.** When you're planning a project, switch to the Free Canvas (third icon in Card View), spread your related cards out, and arrange them how your brain thinks about them. Positions are saved, so you can come back to that arrangement.

**Snooze cards for meetings you're waiting on.** If you're blocked on someone else's response, snooze the card for a few hours. When it resurfaces, you'll be reminded to follow up without any external reminder system.

**Archive, don't delete.** It's tempting to delete completed cards to keep things tidy, but archiving them keeps a history you might want later. Archive View (⌘5) is low-traffic — it won't clutter your working view.

**"Delete completed tasks" for a clean slate.** When you finish a productive sprint, right-click the card and use "Delete completed tasks" to clear out the done items. The card stays, but the noise is gone.

**Carry Forward instead of editing.** At the end of a day or week, you often have a card with a mix of done and not-done tasks. Instead of editing that card, right-click it and choose **Carry Forward**. 3by5 creates a fresh card dated today containing only the unfinished tasks, with all the detail (priority, due date, tags) intact. The original card is untouched — a natural record of what you planned. Think of it as a cleaner version of crossing things off and rewriting them on a new card.

**The dot indicator in Stack View is a jump menu.** If you have a lot of cards and you know roughly where the one you want is in the deck, click a dot near that position instead of flipping one by one.

**Sort Tag column + search = a lightweight tag filter.** Until a dedicated tag filter lands, you can get close: open the Task List, sort by Tag, and then type the tag name in the search field to narrow it down to just that tag's tasks.

---

## A note on capture vs. organisation

The physical 3×5 card was invented as a capture tool — grab a card, write the thing down, deal with it later. 3by5 inherits that philosophy. The goal is to make capturing fast and frictionless (new card with ⌘N, type, done), and to let organisation happen when you have the headspace for it.

If your Later list gets long, that's fine. It means you're capturing well. The weekly review is the moment to cull it back. Not every day.
