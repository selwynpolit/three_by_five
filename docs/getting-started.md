# Getting Started with 3by5

Welcome to 3by5 — a macOS task manager that looks and feels like a stack of real index cards on your desk. No dashboards, no endless settings panels, no productivity guilt. Just cards, tasks, and a satisfying sense of forward motion.

This guide walks you through your first few minutes with the app. By the end, you'll have everything set up and know your way around.


## Your first look

When you open 3by5 for the first time, you're not dropped into an empty screen. A "Personal" stack is already waiting for you, complete with a "Getting Started" card that has six real demo tasks — three in the **Now** column and three in **Later**. Go ahead and poke around; nothing will break.

The card is displayed in **Stack view** (the default), which mimics a physical flip-deck: the active card sits on the left like the top of a draw pile, and cards you've already reviewed slide to the right into a discard pile. Flip through them with the left/right arrow keys, a trackpad swipe, or the navigation arrows on screen. The dots at the bottom show you where you are in the deck — click any dot to jump straight to that card.


## The sidebar: Stacks

On the left you'll see a sidebar with a **STACKS** section. Think of a Stack as a project, context, or area of your life — "Work", "Home", "Side Project", whatever makes sense to you.

At the very top of the list is **All Stacks**, a master row that shows cards from every stack at once. It's great for a morning review when you want the full picture.

To create your first real stack:

1. Hover over the **STACKS** header in the sidebar — a small **+** icon appears.
2. Click it, type a name, and pick one of the 8 color swatches.
3. Hit Create. Your new stack appears in the list immediately.

Each stack row shows a colored dot (that's your chosen color), the stack name, and a checkbox on the left. The checkbox is a **visibility toggle**: checked means that stack's cards are visible in the main view, unchecked means they're temporarily hidden. It's a handy way to focus — uncheck everything except the stack you're working on right now.

When you hover over a stack row, a small **×** button appears on the right. That's the delete button. If you delete a stack, don't worry about losing its cards — they stay accessible through **All Stacks**.


## Creating your first card

Press **⌘N** anywhere in the app (or click the "New Card" button in the top-right header) to create a new card.

Each card has:

- A **date** at the top, shown in a handwritten font (Caveat) that gives it that analog feel.
- An optional **project title** just below the date.
- Two columns: **Now** (things that need your attention today) and **Later** (things that matter but can wait).

The date header isn't just decoration — cards are sorted by date, most recent first, so your newest thinking always floats to the top of the deck.


## Four ways to view your cards

The icons in the top-left header let you switch between four layout modes:

- **Stack view** (default) — the flip-deck experience described above. Great for focused review.
- **Grid** — all your cards tiled side by side. Good for a quick overview.
- **Free Canvas** — drag cards anywhere on an open canvas. Useful for spatial thinkers who like to cluster related cards together.
- **Task List** — a straight list of all tasks across cards. Good when you just want to see everything at once.

You can switch modes any time without losing anything.


## Undoing mistakes

Made an error? 3by5 has your back with a friendly **Undo toast** that appears at the bottom of the screen whenever you hide, snooze, archive, or delete a card — or complete or delete a task. You have four seconds to click **Undo** before the action becomes permanent. It's a small thing, but it makes the app feel forgiving rather than precious.


## The Kanban view

Press **⌘2** (or choose "Kanban view" from a card's right-click menu) to switch to the Kanban board, which shows all your tasks organized in columns by status. If you've used Trello or Linear, you'll feel right at home.


## A quick tour checklist

Here's a natural order to explore things:

1. Flip through the Getting Started card using the arrow keys.
2. Press **⌘N** to create a new card and give it a project title.
3. Add a task to the Now column.
4. Click that task to open the Task Detail panel (slides in from the right).
5. Hover over the STACKS header and create a new stack with your favorite color.
6. Right-click a card header to see the full context menu.

That's genuinely all you need to get going. The rest — due dates, tags, rich notes, attachments — is there when you need it. No pressure to use everything at once.
